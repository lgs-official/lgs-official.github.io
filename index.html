02 · The Image Pipeline

The AI proposes the shot. The camera proves it.

A hybrid workflow built for one non-negotiable: the product is real. Generation designs the world and the light — then the light is rebuilt physically and the actual bottle is shot into it.

FIG. 02 · THE REVERSE-MATCH LOOP

Every image starts with the scent — notes decomposed into palette, material, atmosphere, story — until the brief reads like a lighting plot. The real bottle, shot clean on white, is locked as geometry ground truth, and the hero frame is generated against it with composition and light authored in the prompt: key, fill, kicker, colour temperature.

Then the move that defines the studio: the generated frame becomes the lighting diagram. The plot is physically rebuilt in studio and the real bottle is photographed to match the AI frame’s perspective, focal length, and light. Photoshop composites truth into the world — edge, grain, colour, reflection — and retouch holds glass, liquid, and label to catalogue standard. The product is never AI-approximated, only AI-staged — and none of it is solo work. It’s a directed crew, end to end.

03 · The Video Pipeline

Generated shot by shot. Cut like film.

No single-prompt films. Coverage is planned, generated per shot, reviewed as dailies, and finished with the same post discipline as a live-action commercial.

FIG. 03 · THE VIDEO PIPELINE

A film begins as a written treatment and beat sheet — world, character, arc, tempo — directed on paper before a frame is generated. The white-background bottle anchors per-shot keyframes, and every shot is generated individually, image-to-video, camera language specified per shot, the model chosen per job — Kling 3.0 · Seedance 2.0 · Veo — the way you’d choose a lens. Takes are reviewed as dailies, circle takes selected, while locked references hold character and product identity across shots generated hours apart.

Selects conform to the boarded timeline in DaVinci Resolve for a node-based grade — a continuity pass first, matching shots from different generations into one photographic reality, then the look pass: film-referenced grade, grain, texture. After Effects carries paint-outs, real-bottle composites into generated footage, type and atmosphere; sound is built in layered stems — ambience, foley, transitions, score. The 4K master fans out to 16:9 · 9:16 · 1:1 · 4:5, re-framed per ratio, never centre-cropped.

04 · Craft Transfer & 3D

Teaching the machine to see like a cinematographer.

Generative tools have no taste and no memory — left alone, lens logic wanders, light stops being motivated, brands blur. The answer isn’t better prompts. It’s architecture that encodes twelve years of set knowledge.

FIG. 04 · THE CRAFT STACK

340+ versioned skills hard-code director and DP decisions — focal-length grammar per shot type, motivated-light rules and ratios, blocking logic, pacing, grade discipline — so a generation cannot leave the system without obeying them. Above the skills sits a crew of defined-role agents — direction, look development, continuity, QC — each built to catch a specific kind of drift.

Per-brand style bibles keep three distinct visual signatures separated inside one pipeline — enforced by the system, not by memory, with QC gates on product accuracy, continuity, and grade at every stage. The frontier is 3D: Blender driven agent-first for product-accurate builds, Unreal for cinematic camera work — a hero asset that is absolute product truth, repeatable forever, from every angle.

The tools will change every six months. The craft encoded above them won’t.
